# Translation studies

Translation moves a text between languages, and every choice a translator makes
is an argument about what the text is for. A version that keeps the sound of the
original will lose some of its sense. A version that keeps the sense will read as
prose written yesterday rather than as a poem written in another century.
Scholars call the first choice foreignising and the second domesticating. Neither
is correct on its own. The interesting question is what the translator wanted the
reader to feel, and whether the printed page delivers it.
